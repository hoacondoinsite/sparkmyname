Law 1

One type scale. Seven steps. Nothing else.

16px base, 1.25 ratio. Body is the accessibility floor, never a design choice. Hierarchy comes from weight as much as size — so the scale can stay calm while the page still feels bold.

Law 2

Eleven colours. Three do the work.

Surfaces carry 60% of the screen, panels 30%, accent 10%. Semantic colours signal status only — they never become brand colours, and never fill a large area.

Law 3

Five shapes, and they scale.

Small things get small corners, big things get big corners, actions are pills. Nested corners follow one formula: inner = outer − padding.

Law 4

One primary action per view.

Every action is a pill — the shape you already picked as right. One is filled; the rest are outlines. Three visible maximum, then an overflow. Tabs are not buttons and must never look like them.

What stays

Three rules I did not throw out.

  • ✓The Truth Standard. Nothing on screen may claim more than the code actually does. This is not an aesthetic rule — it is what keeps you honest with paying customers.
  • ✓™ everywhere, ® nowhere. The application is pending, not registered. Using ® early is unlawful and can jeopardise the filing.
  • ✓Small tested batches. One coherent change per disc, tested before it ships. This one has broken checkout before.

Everything else — the 12px rule, the old scale, the banned palette — is gone. What replaces it is above, and it is short enough to hold in your head.
