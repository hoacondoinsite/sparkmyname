## PART ONE — THE EIGHT RULES

These override any instinct, preference, or improvement Claude may be tempted toward.

**1. USE WHAT IS DELIVERED, UNCHANGED.**
The art department returns finished work through API calls: the logo suite (Primary,
Icon, Wordmark), favicons, the color palette, taglines, copy, and photos. These are
**placed, never processed.** No background removal. No re-tracing. No re-deriving. No
re-sharpening. No re-coloring. If artwork looks wrong in a composition, the placement is
wrong — never the artwork.

**2. PALETTE FROM THE BRAND CARD. LOGO ON CLEAN NEUTRAL.**
The colors on the brand card are the only colors. Never sample colors off a logo image.
Never harmonize, substitute, or improve them. The logo suite and the palette come from
two separate API calls and may not match each other — this is expected and is not a
problem to solve. It is handled by keeping them apart: **the logo sits on white or clean
neutral space**, and the palette does its work everywhere else — bands, blocks, headline
color, rules, buttons, and backgrounds the logo does not occupy.

**3. PHOTOS APPEAR IN FULL.**
Every photo is shown in its entirety, scaled proportionately. Nothing cropped off,
nothing stretched, nothing lost. **If the photo does not fit the space, the space
changes — never the photo.** Build the photo's area to the photo's own aspect ratio.

**4. ONE PHOTO PER PIECE, CONFINED TO ITS AREA.**
Seven photos are supplied per brand (one header, six 2K). Each deliverable is assigned a
specific photo in its spec line. No photo repeats while an unused photo remains. A photo
is clipped to its region and may never paint outside the box it was given.

**5. TYPE GOES ON CLEAN SPACE, NOT ON PHOTOGRAPHS.**
Default: type sits beside the image, below it, or on a solid color block from the
palette. Where a piece genuinely requires type over image (hero, story, cover), it needs
a real legibility treatment — a scrim or strip sized to the text block, not a wash over
the whole picture — and must meet 4.5:1 contrast for normal text, 3:1 for large text
(18pt, or 14pt bold).

**6. THE WORDS FIT THE JOB.**
Copy is written for what that specific piece does in the real world, drawn from the
brand's own taglines and supplied information. A yard sign says what a yard sign says. A
thank-you card says what a thank-you card says. No generic filler repeated across pieces.

**7. NOTHING CLIPS. NOTHING IS INVENTED.**
All text fits inside its bounds — if it cannot fit at minimum size, the layout is wrong.
Fields for the client's own details are labeled plainly ("Your phone," "Your name").
Never fabricate names, phone numbers, addresses, or prices.

**8. LOOK BEFORE CALLING IT DONE.**
Open every rendered file and look at it with actual eyes. A spec table is not a
substitute for looking. No deliverable is declared passing without being seen.

**METHOD.** Each deliverable's spec line — dimensions, logo concept and placement,
assigned photo, type placement, and copy — is written and approved *before* rendering.

## PART TWO — WHICH LOGO GOES WHERE

The suite arrives with three concepts. Each has a job. Use the one built for the job;
never shrink a logo below its legible size and never modify one to fit.

| Concept | Use for |
|---|---|
| **Primary** (mark + wordmark) | Large placements: covers, signage, letterhead, cards, presentation covers, avatars with room |
| **Icon** (mark, compact) | Small and square: favicons, tight ad boxes, stamps, anywhere the wordmark would fall below legible size |
| **Wordmark** (name only) | Wide horizontal spaces: lockups, banner strips, footers |

**Clear space.** Measure from the logo's own height (X). Keep at least 0.4–0.5X of empty
margin on every side, free of type, photos, patterns, folds, and page edges.

**Minimum size.** Every logo has a size below which its details and wordmark stop
reading. Below that threshold, switch to the Icon — do not shrink the Primary.

**Never** stretch, squash, rotate, recolor, add effects to, or place a logo on a busy
background.

## PART THREE — PER-ITEM SPECIFICATIONS

### Universal print standards
- **300 DPI** at final size
- **0.125″ bleed** on all four sides
- **Safe area 0.125–0.25″** inside trim — all text and logos live inside it
- CMYK for commercial press; sRGB accepted by online printers
- Headlines/names: at least 4mm from trim, 9–12pt minimum
- Body text: 8–9pt minimum, never below 6pt
- QR codes: 5mm from trim, with a 4-module quiet zone
- Hairline rules: no thinner than 0.25pt

### Universal signage law
**1 inch of capital letter height = 10 feet of readable distance** (United States Sign
Council standard). If the sign is read from a moving vehicle: **seven words maximum**,
bold sans-serif, high contrast, generous margins.

---

### BUSINESS CARD — 3.5 × 2 in
- Trim 1050 × 600 px · Bleed 1125 × 675 px · Safe area 975 × 525 px
- Front and back use identical safe-zone insets
- **Front copy:** logo, brand name, tagline
- **Back copy:** contact name, title, phone, email, domain
- Photo: none

### LETTERHEAD — 8.5 × 11 in
- Logo top-left or top-center; contact top-right or in footer
- Body area left entirely clear for the client's letter
- **Copy:** identity and contact only
- Photo: none

### FLYER / PROPERTY SHEET — 8.5 × 11 in
- One message, one call to action
- Hierarchy through size, weight, spacing and placement together
- Related information grouped; short line lengths; nothing in the margins
- Detail overflow directs to website, never crammed on the page
- **Copy:** headline · 2–3 sentence description · fact row · contact · CTA
- Photo: one, top band, at its own aspect ratio

### TRI-FOLD BROCHURE — 11 × 8.5 in, 3 panels
- Modular grid; content kept clear of fold lines and trim edges
- Panels planned in reveal sequence (cover → inside → back)
- **Copy:** cover identity · about panel · offerings panel · contact panel

### THANK-YOU / REFERRAL / GIFT CARDS
- Card stock sizes; minimal type; one warm sentence; contact line
- Photo: none — these are typographic pieces

### YARD SIGN — 24 × 18 in landscape (industry default)
- Width allows logo beside name, tagline, and phone
- 18 × 24 for quiet streets with close setback
- 24 × 30 or 24 × 36 for fast roads or deep setbacks
- Phone at 3″ letter height reads from ~30 ft; scale by the 1″ = 10 ft law
- **Copy:** headline (FOR SALE / OPEN HOUSE / the offer) · name · phone. Seven words max.
- Photo: none — distance signage is type and logo only

### A-FRAME / SIDEWALK SIGN — 24 × 36 in typical
- Read at walking or slow-driving pace: 2–3″ letters minimum
- **Copy:** what · when · where · who. Short lines.

### VEHICLE MAGNET
- 10 × 20 in for passenger cars · 12 × 24 in for SUVs and pickups · 18 × 24 in and up for vans
- Must read from 20–30 ft
- **Copy:** logo · one line of what the business does · phone
- Photo: none

### EDDM MAILER — strictest rules in the set
- Must **exceed** 6.125″ in height **or** 10.5″ in length. 4×6 and 6×9 do not qualify.
- Maximum 12 × 15 in. Length ÷ height must fall between **1.3 and 2.5**.
- Address block clear area: at least **4″ wide × 1.625″ tall**
- Indicia: minimum 0.5″ square, ALL CAPS, within 1.625″ of the right edge and 1.375″
  from the top, standing alone with nothing crowding it
- **The bottom 2.125″ of the address face must be completely clear** — USPS barcode zone.
  Any graphic, text, or dark background there gets the mailing rejected.
- Common compliant sizes: 6.5 × 9 · 6.25 × 11 · 8.5 × 11 · 9 × 12
- **Copy:** headline offer · 1–2 supporting lines · contact · (address face stays clean)

### SOCIAL — FEED POST — 1080 × 1350 (4:5)
- Portrait is the 2026 native format; it occupies more screen than square
- **Copy:** hook line · 1–2 supporting lines · CTA
- Photo: one, in its own band at true ratio

### SOCIAL — STORY — 1080 × 1920 (9:16)
- **Top 250 px and bottom 340 px must stay free** of text and logos — platform UI covers them
- All content lives in the central band
- **Copy:** short. Three lines maximum.

### SOCIAL — COVER PHOTO
- Facebook cover 851 × 315 · X header 1500 × 500
- Crops differently on desktop and mobile: **design center-weighted**, treat far edges as expendable
- **Copy:** brand name · tagline · domain

### SOCIAL — PROFILE PICTURE
- Upload 720 × 720 or larger (Facebook minimum display 180 × 180)
- Displays as a **circle** — keep the mark centered with generous margin
- Logo: Primary if it reads at size; Icon if not

### SHARE / OG IMAGE — 1200 × 630 (1.91:1)
- The link-preview card for Facebook, LinkedIn, X, iMessage, Slack
- **Copy:** brand name · tagline · domain

### YOUTUBE CHANNEL ART — 2560 × 1440
- All content must sit inside the central **1546 × 423** safe area — everything outside
  is cropped on phones and TVs

### FAVICON — 16 / 32 / 48 / 180 px
- Use the **delivered favicons** if the art department supplied them
- If generating: use the **Icon** concept, contained, never the Primary

### EMAIL SIGNATURE
- **300–400 px wide · 150–200 px tall**
- Images under 100 KB each
- Logo displayed at 150–300 px width, saved at 2× resolution
- Maximum 2 columns (3 absolute limit)
- **Copy:** name · title · company · phone · email · domain

## PART FOUR — WHAT WENT WRONG BEFORE (so it does not repeat)

Recorded from the July 23–24, 2026 session. Every item below was a real failure found by
the Founder in delivered client files.

| Failure | Cause | Rule that prevents it |
|---|---|---|
| Logos fuzzy, "used to look beautiful" | Background-removal + six-step downscale applied to finished art | Rule 1 |
| Favicon rebuilt worse than the delivered one | Spec said "favicons from vector" — regenerating what already existed | Rule 1 |
| Wrong colors (navy/gold instead of the card's palette) | Colors sampled off a logo image instead of read from the brand card | Rule 2 |
| Agent's head cut off in a cover | Center-crop of a wide photo into a narrow band | Rule 3 |
| Photo painted across the copy area | Photo scaled to cover but never clipped to its region | Rule 4 |
| Type unreadable over a photo | Text placed on a busy image with no treatment | Rule 5 |
| Same photo on eleven pieces | No photo assigned per deliverable | Rule 4 |
| Text identical across different pieces | Copy not written for each piece's job | Rule 6 |
| Broken files declared "PASS" | Code gates run; files never opened and looked at | Rule 8 |

**The structural lesson:** every one of these was "fixed" at the time by adding a new
processing step — trace it, plate it, step it, sample it. Each addition created the next
failure. **The correction is subtraction.** The art arrives finished. Place it.

## PART FIVE — DEFERRED (do not improvise)

These are real needs that do **not** have researched answers yet. Do not invent rules for
them. Ask the Founder.

- **Occasion and holiday variants** — a client's Easter, Christmas, or grand-opening
  piece may leave the brand palette. Triggered by the client's request or the occasion,
  never by Claude's taste. Rules to be written when studied.
- **Client customization fields** — the industry pattern is a governed template: brand
  elements locked, named zones left editable. To be specified later.
- **Additional photo calls** — permitted for special occasions the seven supplied photos
  cannot serve. Not for basics.
